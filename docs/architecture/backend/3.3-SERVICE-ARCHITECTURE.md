# Phase 3.3 — Service Architecture

## Status

Proposed — derived from the frozen enterprise architecture, ADR set, Phase 2 information architecture, and Phase 3.1–3.2 database architecture.

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the backend service boundaries for Essentials Mart. Services are aligned to enterprise domains and aggregate ownership rather than UI screens or database tables.

A service owns the commands, invariants, transactions, and persistence associated with its domain. Other services interact through explicit APIs, commands, events, and read models. No service may directly mutate another service's owned state.

## 2. Architectural Rules

1. One authoritative owner exists for every mutable business capability.
2. Aggregate invariants are enforced inside the owning domain boundary.
3. Database ownership follows service ownership; shared write tables are prohibited.
4. Cross-service workflows use commands and events rather than distributed database transactions.
5. Queries may use dedicated read models where operational aggregates should not be exposed directly.
6. External providers are isolated behind adapters owned by the relevant service.
7. AI agents do not bypass service boundaries or write directly to operational storage.
8. Authentication and authorization are enforced at the service boundary and again at sensitive domain operations.
9. Idempotency is mandatory for externally retried commands and event consumers.
10. Service boundaries may be deployed together initially; logical ownership must exist before physical separation.

## 3. Core Commerce Services

### 3.1 Cart Service

Owns:
- Cart aggregate
- Cart items
- cart lifecycle and expiry
- cart mutations
- cart totals that are valid within the cart context

Commands include:
- CreateCart
- AddCartItem
- RemoveCartItem
- UpdateCartItemQuantity
- ClearCart
- InitiateCheckout

Publishes domain events such as CartChanged and CheckoutInitiated.

### 3.2 Checkout Service

Owns:
- Checkout Session
- checkout state machine
- frozen purchase snapshot
- discount/tax/shipping calculation orchestration
- checkout idempotency
- coordination of inventory reservation and payment initiation

Checkout does not own Cart, Product, Inventory, Order, or Payment state.

### 3.3 Order Service

Owns:
- Order aggregate
- Order items and historical snapshots
- order lifecycle
- fulfillment contract
- order state transitions

It consumes authoritative payment and fulfillment events rather than reaching into Payment or Fulfillment storage.

### 3.4 Payment Service

Owns:
- Payment Transaction
- payment attempts
- authorization/capture state
- PSP integration adapters
- webhook processing
- payment idempotency

Payment state is authoritative for financial settlement status. It does not mutate Order directly; it publishes events consumed by Order.

### 3.5 Refund Service

Owns:
- Refund aggregate
- refund requests
- approval workflow
- partial refund state
- refund settlement lifecycle
- store-credit refund workflows where applicable

Refund references Order and Payment identifiers but owns its own state and lifecycle.

## 4. Catalog, Store and Inventory Services

### 4.1 Product Catalog Service

Owns product identity, descriptions, pricing authority, availability metadata, categories, and product lifecycle.

Commerce services reference ProductId and obtain immutable purchase snapshots. Commerce never mutates catalog state.

### 4.2 Store Service

Owns:
- stores
- departments
- aisles
- shelves/locations
- store operating configuration
- store-specific layout metadata

This becomes a primary source for Walk Mode's physical-store representation.

### 4.3 Inventory Service

Owns:
- inventory position
- stock levels
- inventory reservations/holds
- reservation expiry
- stock adjustments
- inventory movement records

Checkout requests reservations; Inventory determines whether the reservation can be created. Reservation lifecycle is authoritative within Inventory.

## 5. Fulfillment and Delivery Services

### 5.1 Fulfillment Service

Owns:
- picking
- packing
- fulfillment tasks
- substitutions
- fulfillment status

### 5.2 Delivery Service

Owns:
- delivery orders
- delivery assignment
- dispatch state
- delivery tracking
- delivery-provider integrations
- delivery completion/failure

Fulfillment and Delivery are distinct because warehouse/store execution and transport have different operational lifecycles.

## 6. Customer, Household and Identity Services

### 6.1 Identity Service

Owns authentication identities, credentials/provider identities, sessions, authorization context, and security principals.

### 6.2 Customer Service

Owns customer profile and customer-facing preferences that are not household state.

### 6.3 Household Service

Owns household membership, household roles, shared preferences, household plans, and household-level state.

A user's identity is not the same thing as a household membership.

## 7. Communication Services

### Notification Service

Owns notification intents, templates, delivery preferences, in-app notification state, and channel orchestration.

### WhatsApp Integration Service

Owns WhatsApp-specific transport, session handling, message normalization, consent state required for the integration, and provider interaction.

Business capabilities remain owned by their source services; WhatsApp is a channel, not a second business domain.

## 8. AI Society Services

### AI Orchestration Service

Coordinates agent invocation, task decomposition, permissions, context assembly, tool access, escalation, and human-in-the-loop controls.

### Intelligence Services

Specialized Intelligence Engines remain capability boundaries rather than independent owners of enterprise truth. Examples include:
- Recommendation Intelligence
- Pricing Intelligence
- Inventory Intelligence
- Demand Forecasting
- Fraud/Risk Intelligence
- Personalization Intelligence
- Delivery Intelligence

They consume authorized data and return decisions/recommendations to owning services. They do not directly mutate another domain's operational state.

## 9. Enterprise and Risk Services

### Risk/Fraud Service

Owns fraud assessments, risk signals, cases, rules, and risk decisions.

### Audit Service

Owns immutable audit records and decision-trace records required by the observability, auditability, and trust architecture.

### Enterprise Intelligence Service

Provides enterprise-level read models, analytics and decision support. It does not become the operational owner of domain data.

## 11. Checkout Coordination

The checkout workflow is explicitly a process boundary rather than a distributed transaction:

1. Cart requests checkout initiation.
2. Checkout creates a Checkout Session and freezes the relevant purchase snapshot.
3. Checkout requests Inventory to create a time-bound reservation.
4. Checkout requests Payment to initiate/authorize payment.
5. Successful payment produces an authoritative PaymentSucceeded event.
6. Order consumes the event and transitions according to its state machine.
7. Inventory converts or releases the reservation according to the resulting business outcome.
8. Fulfillment begins from the resulting Order state.
9. Failures/expiry publish compensating events rather than attempting cross-database rollback.

| Service | Primary owned state |
|---|---|
| Identity | identities, sessions, credentials/provider identities |
| Customer | customer profiles/preferences |
| Household | households, memberships, household state |
| Product Catalog | products, catalog metadata, prices |
| Store | stores, layouts, locations |
| Inventory | stock, reservations, inventory movements |
| Cart | carts, cart items |
| Checkout | checkout sessions, frozen snapshots, checkout state |
| Order | orders, order items, order state |
| Payment | payment transactions, attempts |
| Refund | refunds and refund state |
| Fulfillment | picking/packing/fulfillment tasks |
| Delivery | deliveries, assignments, tracking |
| Notification | notification records and preferences |

| Audit | audit and decision-trace records |
| AI Orchestration | agent tasks, executions, permissions/context records |

## 13. What Must Not Happen

The following patterns are prohibited:

- A service directly updating another service's tables.
- AI agents writing directly to domain databases.
- Checkout directly changing inventory quantities.
- Payment directly changing Order status through a database write.
- Notification services becoming owners of business decisions.
- WhatsApp handlers containing commerce business logic.
- Analytics/read models becoming authoritative operational stores.
- A shared database transaction spanning unrelated aggregate owners merely for convenience.

## 14. Initial Deployment Strategy

The architecture does not require every logical service to become a separate deployable process immediately. Early implementation may use a modular monolith or a small number of deployables while preserving the logical service boundaries in code, schemas, interfaces, and ownership rules.

This allows Essentials Mart to scale operationally without prematurely paying the complexity cost of distributed deployment.

## 15. Phase 3.3 Exit Criteria

Phase 3.3 is complete when:

- every Phase 2 aggregate has an explicit owner;
- every mutable capability has one authoritative service;
- cross-service interactions are defined as commands, queries, or events;
- database ownership is aligned with service ownership;
- checkout coordination is explicitly modeled without distributed transactions;
- AI Society access respects service boundaries;
- notification and channel integrations remain downstream capabilities;
- the architecture can be translated into API contracts in Phase 3.4 without inventing new domain ownership.
